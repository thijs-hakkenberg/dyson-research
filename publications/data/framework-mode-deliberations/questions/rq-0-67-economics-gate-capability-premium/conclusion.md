## Summary

Claude Opus 5.5, Gemini 3.1 Pro and GPT-6.1 Sol converged on **separate, explicit owner authorisation for any above-Earth-supply capability premium**. All three rejected leaving the original threshold dispute unresolved. All three also retained a route for capability investment above parity rather than making cost savings an absolute prerequisite. GPT stated most explicitly that D-01 establishes programme purpose but does not itself authorise a subsidy.

The panel did **not** converge on how an authorised premium should affect the gate finding or when it may be approved. Claude would pre-authorise a bounded premium, normally by the 0A mid-term review, and permit a premium-authorised gate pass while reporting parity separately. Gemini and GPT would retain an adverse economics finding and record any decision to proceed separately; both allow that authorisation at KDP-0B. The votes supported the broad separation of cost assessment from owner authority, but preserved objections concerning late threshold changes, Claude’s proposed exceptions, and the calibration of cost criteria.

## Decision record

**Held by Claude Opus 5.5**

> In the context of D-01’s capability programme and D-02’s irreversible-gate discipline, facing uncertain costs and the risk of thresholds being adjusted after results are known, we chose a parity assessment combined with a separately authorised, pre-committed capability premium, to achieve a discriminating gate and explicit owner accountability, accepting reduced flexibility to credit late-discovered benefits.

- **Warrant — Claude:** A threshold selected after the evidence is known ceases to function as a meaningful test.
- **Operational commitment — Claude:** Default to \(R=1\); require a separate owner decision setting a ratio ceiling, an absolute incremental-dollar cap and named benefits. Normally approve this by the 0A mid-term review.
- **Qualifier — Claude:** Recorded amendments could occur before assembly of the KDP-0B package. Claude also proposed special treatment for low-launch-price scenarios and market-backed premiums.
- **Accepted downside — Claude:** Late benefits cannot be credited without an explicit amendment; absent authorisation, parity applies.
- **Reservation — GPT:** Those amendment and exemption provisions weaken Claude’s stated pre-commitment.

### Position 2 — Premium authorisation does not convert an economics failure into a pass  
**Held by Gemini 3.1 Pro and GPT-6.1 Sol**

> In the context of a capability programme approaching an irreversible commitment, facing unresolved authority for above-parity expenditure, we chose a parity-based economics finding with a separately recorded owner decision for any premium, to achieve transparent cost assessment and accountable strategic investment, accepting additional approval work and the possibility of proceeding despite an adverse economics finding.

- **Warrant — Gemini and GPT:** Capability value may justify additional expenditure, but that justification should not be presented as cost competitiveness.
- **Timing — Gemini and GPT:** The premium decision may be taken at KDP-0B; GPT explicitly allowed the same meeting, provided the findings remain distinct.
- **Qualifier — GPT:** The ruling could change if the full accepted decisions already grant bounded premium authority, or if Earth supply is not a valid equivalent-service comparator.
- **Accepted downside — Gemini and GPT:** Additional governance friction; GPT also acknowledged the discomfort of approving a strategically worthwhile project with a disclosed economics failure.
- **Reservation — Claude:** Approval after the cost evidence is available leaves an escalation-of-commitment route open.

## Key Points

- **Separate authority is the common ground.** Claude, Gemini and GPT all required an explicit owner decision for a capability premium; none treated D-01 alone as sufficient approval.
- **The original undercut position was withdrawn by Gemini.** Gemini’s round-one recommendation used parity with a separate premium route. GPT rejected mandatory undercutting without a justified margin; Claude also rejected it.
- **Absolute exposure matters alongside ratios.** Claude proposed both ratio and dollar ceilings; GPT required an absolute premium limit and total funding envelope. GPT cautioned that a ratio does not replace either.
- **The cost statistic remains unsettled.** Claude retained project P70 versus Earth P50. GPT proposed comparable expected costs plus a separate P70 funding envelope and downside limits. Gemini did not resolve that distinction in equivalent detail.
- **The reported estimate spread is not a calibrated probability distribution.** GPT explicitly warned against that inference. Claude and GPT both called for estimate reconciliation.
- **Other gates remain independent.** GPT explicitly stated that premium approval cannot waive target, demand, licence or D-02 technical requirements.
- **Scenario policy remains open.** Claude proposed a low-launch-price exception to the ratio ceiling. GPT instead recommended a non-contingent pass across predeclared core scenarios, or a conditional finding with enforceable triggers.

## Unresolved Questions

1. **Gate semantics:** Should an authorised premium produce Claude’s premium-authorised pass, or Gemini/GPT’s economics failure plus separate investment approval?
2. **Authorisation timing:** Should the owner commit by the 0A mid-term review, as Claude proposed, or retain a distinct KDP-0B decision, as Gemini and GPT allowed?
3. **Cost criterion:** Should the gate use Claude’s P70/P50 comparison or GPT’s expected-cost comparison with separate downside controls?
4. **Caps and exceptions:** Should Claude’s market-backed exemption and low-launch-price ratio exception be retained despite GPT’s objections?
5. **Readiness standard:** What confidence definition and validation support an estimate band such as Claude’s ±30% proposal? GPT disputed treating an undefined band as assurance.
6. **Existing authority and comparator:** Does the full accepted decision text already authorise premiums, and can equivalent Earth supply be specified? GPT identified both as potential ruling-changing evidence.

- **Resolve the remaining governance choices explicitly:** choose the timing and gate-label policy rather than leaving them implicit in \(R\), reflecting the competing Claude and Gemini/GPT proposals.
